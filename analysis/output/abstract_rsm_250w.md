# Abstract (Target: Research Synthesis Methods)

## Background
Meta-analyses are often interpreted as comparably reliable despite differing levels of assumption strain. We evaluated whether a composite assumption-burden measure ("assumption debt") is associated with fragility of meta-analytic conclusions in Cochrane pairwise meta-analyses.

## Methods
We analyzed 4,424 pairwise meta-analyses from Pairwise70 (474 review datasets). The primary outcome was any fragility (direction- or significance-fragile). The primary model regressed fragility on assumption-debt score (0-4) adjusted for log(k), absolute effect size, and tau2 (capped at 99th percentile). We used dataset-clustered robust standard errors and a prespecified robustness multiverse spanning outcome definitions, link functions, weighting schemes, dependence-sensitive designs, threshold sweeps, and conservative null checks.

## Results
Fragility rose monotonically with score (32.0% at score 0 to 70.0% at score 3). Per +1 score point, odds of fragility increased (OR 1.15, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.28; cluster-robust OR 1.15, 95% CI 1.02 to 1.30). Direction was preserved in assessability-focused sensitivities, including no-Egger score (cluster-robust OR 1.18, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.34) and assessable-only subset k >= 10 (cluster-robust OR 1.31, 95% CI 1.07 to 1.61). Across the robustness matrix, 51/52 directional checks were positive, 45/52 had confidence intervals excluding the null in the positive direction, and exact sign-test evidence against a 50% directional-null was strong (one-sided p = 1.18e-14). Conservative permutation analysis attenuated inference (empirical p = 0.08).

## Conclusions
Higher assumption debt is consistently associated with higher fragility across a wide range of synthesis specifications. Results support interpreting assumption debt as a practical instability-risk marker for evidence synthesis, while avoiding standalone causal claims.
