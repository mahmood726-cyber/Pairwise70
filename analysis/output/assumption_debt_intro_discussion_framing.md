# Framing Draft: Biggest Underdiagnosed Flaw in Modern Meta-Analysis

## Core Diagnosis (one sentence)
The biggest underdiagnosed flaw is **unmeasured assumption burden**: meta-analyses are interpreted as if outputs are comparably trustworthy even when their underlying assumption load differs substantially.

## Introduction Draft (manuscript-ready)
Modern meta-analysis emphasizes pooled effect estimation and heterogeneity quantification, but it still under-measures the cumulative burden of violated or strained assumptions that make conclusions brittle. This creates a structural blind spot: two analyses can report similar effect sizes while differing materially in inferential stability. We refer to this latent burden as **assumption debt**.

In 4,424 Cochrane pairwise meta-analyses, fragility increased monotonically with assumption-debt score (32.0% at score 0, 38.4% at score 1, 50.9% at score 2, 70.0% at score 3), and each +1 score point was associated with higher fragility odds (primary OR 1.15, cluster-robust OR 1.15). This pattern remained directionally consistent across multiple sensitivity designs, including component outcomes, clinical fragility, and composite fragility-count intensity (cluster-robust RR 1.16). The central methodological problem is therefore not only effect-size estimation error, but **failure to routinely model and report assumption burden as a first-class inferential quantity**.

## Discussion Draft (manuscript-ready)
These findings support a practical reframing: assumption burden should be treated as an explicit risk dimension, not a background caveat. The between-review component of the score effect exceeded the within-review component, and conservative checks (dataset fixed-effects attenuation and within-dataset permutation p = 0.08) indicate that mechanistic attribution should remain cautious. The appropriate interpretation is not that assumption debt is the sole causal driver of fragility, but that it is a robust and actionable marker of instability risk.

For applied evidence synthesis, three reporting changes follow. First, every meta-analysis should include an assumption-debt profile alongside pooled effect results. Second, fragility should be reported with both binary and intensity formulations to avoid loss of information from dichotomization. Third, claims should be calibrated to debt level, with stronger language reserved for low-debt analyses and explicit caution for high-debt analyses. This framework operationalizes epistemic humility: neither overclaiming certainty nor discarding evidence, but ranking trustworthiness by observable assumption stress.

## Short Abstract Insert
Across Cochrane pairwise meta-analyses, the principal underdiagnosed flaw was unmeasured assumption burden: higher assumption debt consistently tracked greater fragility, supporting routine debt profiling as a core reporting standard.
